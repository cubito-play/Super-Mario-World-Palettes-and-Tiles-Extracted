# Super Mario World – Tiles and Palettes

## About the project

This repository contains tile and palette files extracted from Super Mario World during an independent study and reverse engineering project, carried out exclusively for educational purposes.

The initial goal was to understand how Super Mario World stores, organizes, and uses its graphics. The idea was to develop our own tools capable of extracting the graphic files from the ROM, identifying the tiles, discovering their color palettes, and, in the future, reconstructing the game's characters, objects, and scenarios.

## Why did the project start?

It all began with the curiosity to understand how a classic game like Super Mario World works internally.

The proposal was to go beyond simply visualizing the graphics: we wanted to understand how they were stored, how the tiles were organized, how the palettes were used, and how the game combined all these elements to form the images displayed on the screen.

During development, tools were created for graphic extraction, decompression, tile analysis, palette exploration, and sprite reconstruction.

It was a process of learning, experimentation, and discovery, with an exclusively educational purpose.

## Why was the project terminated?

Despite the progress, the project ended up becoming more complex than expected.

One of the main challenges was correctly organizing the tiles and identifying the color palettes used by each one. It wasn't enough to extract the images; it was necessary to understand how each tile should be interpreted and colored.

There were also more advanced steps ahead, such as capturing graphic data directly from an emulator and comparing the reconstructions with real images from the game.

With the increasing complexity and the work required to complete these steps, I decided to discontinue development.

This doesn't mean the idea has no value or that it's impossible to complete. It simply means that, at this time, I will not continue the project.

## About the provided files

The files in this repository are still under development and do not represent a completely organized set of graphics.

**It's important to know that:**

- The tiles are still disorganized. - The images do not yet have the colors applied correctly. - The tile and palette associations are not finalized.

- Some files may require additional processing.

- This material does not represent a final reconstruction of the game's graphics.

I am making this material available so that other interested people can study it, explore it, and, if they wish, continue from where I left off.

I don't want all the work done so far to simply be forgotten in a folder.

Perhaps someone will be able to solve the problems I found, organize the tiles, identify the correct palettes, and take the idea much further.

## For those who want to continue

If you are interested in reverse engineering, programming, image processing, or are simply curious about how Super Mario World works, feel free to explore the material.

Some ideas to continue the work:

1. Organize the tiles by GFX files.

2. Correctly identify the palettes used.

3. Apply colors to the tiles.

4. Develop tools to visualize and organize the graphics.

5. Investigate how the tiles are used during the game.

6. Try to reconstruct sprites and complete scenarios.

**The project may have stopped here, but nothing prevents someone else from continuing it.**

## Conclusion

This work was developed exclusively for educational purposes, with the goal of teaching programming, graphic formats, image processing, and the inner workings of games.

The files are being made available as study material and a starting point for future experiments.

I thank everyone who was interested in exploring this material and, especially, those who decide to continue the work.

**Good luck to whoever decides to take this project forward!**
